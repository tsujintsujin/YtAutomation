# 1. Getting paid: the rules for a channel with long videos and Shorts

Researched 2026-11-07. Tags: **[YouTube]** = YouTube's own page, read that day. **[report]** = press or a third-party
study. **[creator]** = one creator's claim. **[mine]** = my reading of the facts, not a fact.

## The short version
- **A channel started now will not be accepted before 2027-02-01.** Plan for the new bar: 1,000 subscribers plus
  **8,000 watch hours on long videos in 12 months**, or 20 million Shorts views in 90 days.
- **Long videos are where the money and the watch hours are.** Shorts views never count as watch hours, Shorts pay a
  few percent of what long videos pay, and from 2027 a channel needs 10 million Shorts views every 90 days to be paid
  from Shorts at all. **Shorts are for finding viewers, not for income.**
- **The policy risk is "same story, same ending, every video".** YouTube names that exact pattern as not monetizable.
  Every video must be clearly different and carry real value (a story, facts, an explanation).
- **Cartoon or animated pictures need no AI label.** The narration voice is the open question (below).

## 1.1 The bar: now and from 2027-02-01
**[YouTube]** Full Partner Program (ads), today: 1,000 subscribers + 4,000 qualified watch hours in 12 months, or 1,000
subscribers + 10M qualified Shorts views in 90 days. Watch hours count only **public long videos**; Shorts feed watch
time never counts. Shorts views count only public Shorts seen in the Shorts feed.

**[YouTube]** From **2027-02-01**, new applicants need 1,000 subscribers + **8,000** watch hours (365 days) or **20M**
Shorts views (90 days). Channels already in the program are not affected. The lower tier (fan funding, memberships,
Shopping) is unchanged: 500 subscribers + 3,000 watch hours or 3M Shorts views.

**[YouTube]** Also needed: 2-Step Verification, one AdSense for YouTube account, "advanced features" access, no
Community Guidelines strikes, a supported country. Meeting the numbers starts a review by people; it is not automatic.

**[YouTube]** Review takes "typically about 1 month". First rejection: appeal within 21 days, or keep uploading and
re-apply after 30 days. Second rejection: wait 90 days. YouTube's 2027 page **does not say** what happens to an
application still under review on 2027-02-01.

**[mine] What that means for a new channel:** about 12 weeks remain, minus a month of review. 1,000 subscribers and
4,000 hours in ~8 weeks from zero needs a hit in the first videos. Possible, not a plan. Plan for 8,000 hours.

**8,000 hours, in views [mine]:** 8,000 h = 480,000 minutes.

| Average view duration | Long-video views needed in 12 months |
|---|---|
| 4 min (a 12-min video watched a third) | 120,000 |
| 6 min (a 15-20 min video) | 80,000 |
| 8 min (a 20-25 min video held well) | 60,000 |

For scale: in the POV money niche, brand-new channels had single videos at 100K-660K views in the last month
(`channels/book-stories/STRATEGY.md`). One hit like that covers the whole year. Without a hit, 80-120K views is
~250-330 views a day for a year.

**20M Shorts views in 90 days** = ~222,000 views a day. LuckySticks got ~6,300 views in its first two weeks. This route
only works with Shorts that break out again and again.

**Channel activity rule [YouTube]** (from 2027 for partners): a channel counts as active with 2 long videos or 5 Shorts
every 90 days. **Channels with no uploads for 6 months may lose monetization.**

## 1.2 What Shorts pay
**[YouTube]** Shorts ad money is pooled each month. The pool is shared by each creator's share of engaged Shorts views
per country; the creator keeps 45% of their share. Music in a Short takes part of the pool (one track: half).
**[YouTube] From 2027-02-01, a partner earns from the Shorts pool only in months where it has 10M Shorts views in the
last 90 days.** Long-video earnings are not affected by that.

**[report]** AIR Media-Tech, YouTube Analytics data from 274 channels that post both: Shorts RPM was **3-14% of
long-video RPM in almost every niche** (music is the exception, 41-60%). A second AIR study, 300 channels, May 2025 -
May 2026: median long-video RPM over all niches ~$2.30 per 1,000 views; Education & Science highest at **$10.22**
(middle half $2.31-$19.50). Business & Finance came out at $2.01, but from fewer than 10 channels, so it says little;
blog lists put finance first at $4-30 without measured data. These two studies are the only measured sets found.

**[mine]** Viewer country matters as much as niche: LuckySticks' viewers are 49% Philippines, and PH ads pay far less
than US ones. English titles about US/UK life pull US viewers.

## 1.3 What YouTube will not pay for
**[YouTube] Inauthentic content** (renamed from "repetitious" on 2025-07-15). Not monetizable:
- content that looks made from a template, or feels repetitive when watched several in a row;
- **"characters in the same situation with the same outcome"**, using a highly similar storyline template;
- image slideshows, templated storylines or scrolling text with little or no narrative or educational value;
- AI-made content from generic templates that gives the impression of mass production without the creator's own
  insight;
- channels where videos "feel interchangeable".

Allowed: the same intro/outro; recurring characters or a series, if each video has its own storyline or focus; AI used
to edit scripts, make a unique background, or visualize a character and story you invented; templates and automated
tools, as long as "the final product must still demonstrate your creative vision". The core test: each video's
substance must be "materially varied" and give creative, educational or other value.

**[YouTube] Reused content:** others' material without substantial original commentary or changes, readings of
others' text (articles, news), compilations. Applies even with permission. **Cutting Shorts from our own long videos is
not addressed** on the policy pages; the reviewer's question is whether "you made the content". **[mine]** Shorts cut
from our own original videos are our own work; the risk would only come if the channel looked like the same clips
posted again and again.

**[YouTube] Judged for the whole channel.** Reviewers can't watch everything, so they look at the channel's main theme,
most viewed videos, newest videos, the videos with most watch time, titles/thumbnails/descriptions, and the About
section. A failing pattern costs the whole channel, not one video.

**[report] Enforcement is real.** In January 2026 YouTube removed 16 large AI channels (4.7 billion lifetime views,
35M subscribers combined) under its spam and deceptive-practices policies; YouTube said it was existing policy, not a
new anti-AI rule. Other reports speak of far larger sweeps of "AI slop" channels; those numbers are less reliable.

**[mine] The warning for us:** the POV money formula ("someone underestimated you about money; you were secretly
rich") is one situation with one outcome. Done daily, it is close to YouTube's own "same situation, same outcome"
example. Every video needs a genuinely different premise, turn and ending, and real teaching value (the book lesson
with real numbers helps). The same applies to any niche we pick.

## 1.4 The AI label
**[YouTube]** Must be labeled: AI content that **seems realistic**: a real person saying or doing what they didn't, a
real event or place altered, a realistic scene that never happened, AI music as the main focus.
Not needed: **non-realistic content** (YouTube's examples: a unicorn world, an AI missile in a fully animated video),
and help like AI scripts, outlines, thumbnails, captions, upscaling, **cloning your own voice** for voice-overs.
Labeling does not reduce reach or monetization. Creators who keep not labeling when required can get a forced label,
removed videos, or suspension from the program.

**Open question for us:** the page doesn't cover a text-to-speech narrator, nor a clone of someone else's voice. Our
Chatterbox voice copies the sample "Mark" (`tts/samples/voices/29_mark.mp3`). **[mine]** If that sample is a real
person's voice, the narration is a real person "appearing to say" our words, so either label it or switch to a voice
that is nobody's (or Justin's own, which is explicitly exempt). Our cartoon/stick/animated pictures need no label.

## 1.5 Income before the Partner Program
- **[YouTube]** Lower tier at 500 subscribers + 3,000 hours or 3M Shorts views: fan funding (memberships, Super
  Thanks) and YouTube Shopping. Small for a faceless channel.
- **Affiliate links in descriptions** (for example the money books on a money channel). **[report]** Amazon Associates
  is said to accept people from the Philippines, paid by check, gift card or a US bank via Payoneer; **not checked on
  Amazon's own terms yet**. Needs Justin (an account in his name).
- **[creator]** Jack Craig: a recognizable brand or character makes income outside ads (sponsors, products) far easier.

## 1.6 Decisions this part asks for
1. Target the 2027 bar (8,000 hours) and treat Feb 2027 as a bonus only if a video breaks out.
2. Long videos carry the money and the watch hours; Shorts bring viewers to them.
3. No single premise template: each video's situation, turn and ending must differ.
4. Settle the voice question (whose voice "Mark" is) before the channel launches.
