## vulovic-fall-luck
- Vesna Vulović, flight attendant on JAT Yugoslav Airlines Flight 367, 26 January 1972: she was not scheduled to work that flight (the airline had confused her with another stewardess with the same first name); the plane exploded into three pieces (the Czechoslovak authority said a bomb in the baggage hold); she fell 10,160 m (33,333 ft), the highest fall survived without a parachute; she was pinned by a food cart in the tail end of the fuselage, which landed in thick snow at a favourable angle; everyone else on board was killed; fractured skull, two broken legs, three broken vertebrae, a fractured pelvis, broken ribs; days in a coma; no memory of the crash; she kept working for JAT in a desk job: [Guinness World Records](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/news/2022/4/how-vesna-vulovic-survived-the-highest-fall-ever-with-no-parachute-697786)
- Same story: [This Day in Aviation](https://thisdayinaviation.com/26-january-1972/), [Amusing Planet](https://amusingplanet.com/2022/09/vesna-vulovics-33000-feet-fall.html)

## packard-whale-luck
- Michael Packard (56), a commercial lobster diver from Wellfleet; on 11 June 2021, diving off Herring Cove Beach, Provincetown (Cape Cod), something hit him and everything went black; he first thought it was a shark but felt no teeth; he was inside a humpback whale's mouth for about 30-40 seconds, still breathing from his regulator, thinking of his boys (12 and 15); the whale surfaced, shook its head and spat him out; his crewmate Josiah Mayo pulled him into the boat; he was released from Cape Cod Hospital hours later, saying he had bruises and no broken bones: [NPR / KUOW](https://www.kuow.org/stories/a-lobster-diver-in-cape-cod-says-a-humpback-whale-scooped-him-up-and-spat-him-out), [NBC New York](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/news/national-international/humpback-whale-catches-lobster-diver-in-its-mouth-sending-him-to-hospital/3103037/), [FOX (Cape Cod Times account)](https://www.fox13news.com/news/massachusetts-lobster-diver-survives-being-swallowed-by-whale-i-was-completely-inside), [LBC](https://www.lbc.co.uk/article/lobster-diver-swallowed-humpback-whale-survives-cape-cod-usa-DWyxt4_2/)

## gage-iron-rod-luck
- Phineas Gage, foreman on the Rutland and Burlington Railroad near Cavendish, Vermont; on 13 September 1848, while packing blasting powder into a drilled hole with a long iron tamping rod, a spark set the charge off; the rod went in under his left cheekbone and out the top of his head and landed about 25 m (80 ft) behind him; within minutes he was conscious and speaking, and he rode into town sitting upright in an ox cart; he lived until May 1860 (about 11.7 years); his doctor reported that friends said he was "no longer Gage", though later accounts suggest he recovered more over time: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phineas_Gage), [Vermont History Explorer](https://vermonthistoryexplorer.org/phineas-gage), [Harvard Medical School](https://hms.harvard.edu/news/new-view-phineas-gage), [History UK](https://www.history.co.uk/articles/how-did-phineas-gage-survive-his-brain-injury)

## okene-sunken-ship-luck
- Harrison Okene (29), cook on the tugboat Jascon-4; it capsized in rough seas at about 4:50 a.m. on 26 May 2013 off Nigeria while working at an oil platform, and settled upside down on the sea floor about 30 m down; he was in the toilet; he found an air pocket about four feet high, drank a can of Coca-Cola, and his two flashlights died within a day; divers from DCN Diving, sent to recover bodies, found him alive about 60-62 hours later when he grabbed a diver's hand; he was the only survivor of the 12 crew; he then spent about 60 hours in a decompression chamber: [ABC News](https://abcnews.go.com/blogs/headlines/2013/06/man-survives-nearly-3-days-submerged-in-capsized-boat), [ITV](https://www.itv.com/news/2013-12-02/miraculous-ship-rescue-new-footage-emerges), [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/1006413/watch-amazing-video-of-nigerian-man-being-rescued-from-sunken-tugboat-after-2-days-underwater/), [KSL (AP)](https://www.ksl.com/article/27984259)

## hodges-meteorite-luck
- 30 November 1954, near Sylacauga, Alabama: Ann Hodges was napping on her living-room couch at mid-day when a meteorite of about eight and a half pounds came through the ceiling, hit a console radio and smashed into her hip; her hip was badly bruised. Her landlady, Birdie Guy, sued for the rock; under a settlement she gave up her claim to the Hodges. The meteorite is on permanent display at the Alabama Museum of Natural History, University of Alabama, Tuscaloosa: [Encyclopedia of Alabama](https://encyclopediaofalabama.org/article/hodges-meteorite-strike-sylacauga-aerolite/)
- "The first known human injury via a meteorite": [Astronomy](https://www.astronomy.com/today-in-the-history-of-astronomy/nov-30-1954-the-first-known-human-injury-via-a-meteorite/), [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/130220-russia-meteorite-ann-hodges-science-space-hit), [Space.com](https://space.com/meteorite-hit-alabama-woman-65-years-ago.html)
- Media frenzy after the strike (reporters at the house): Encyclopedia of Alabama and Astronomy above.
- Donated to the museum in 1956: Encyclopedia of Alabama.

## oliver-elevator-luck
- Betty Lou Oliver (20), an elevator operator in the Empire State Building; on 28 July 1945 a US Army B-25 bomber flew into the building in thick fog; she was thrown from her car and badly burned; after first aid she was put in another elevator to be taken down, but its cables had been cut by the crash and it fell about 75 storeys to the sub-basement; the cut cables piled up in a spring-like coil at the bottom and the trapped air also slowed it; she had a broken pelvis, back and neck; Guinness lists it as the longest fall survived in an elevator: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1945_Empire_State_Building_B-25_crash), [Guinness World Records](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/news/2023/10/How-an-Elevator-attendant-Survived-a-1-000-Ft-Fall-Down-the-Empire-State-Building-759670)
- Her return about five months later, when she rode an elevator again: [Elevator World](https://elevatorworld.com/?p=15405), [History Collection](https://historycollection.com/cheat-death-twice-betty-lou-oliver-survived-75-storey-elevator-crash-plane-crashed-building/3/)

## sullivan-lightning-luck
- Roy Sullivan, park ranger in Shenandoah National Park, Virginia; recognised by Guinness World Records as struck by lightning more times than anyone (seven). April 1942 in a fire lookout tower (burned a strip along his right leg, hit his toe, left a hole in his shoe); July 1969 driving his truck on a mountain road (knocked out, eyebrows and eyelashes burned off, hair on fire); July 1970 in his front yard (left shoulder seared); spring 1972 inside a ranger station (hair on fire); 7 August 1973 on patrol (hair on fire, he poured a can of water over his head); 5 June 1976 (ankle hurt, hair on fire); 25 June 1977 fishing in a freshwater pool (hit on top of the head, hair on fire), and a bear tried to steal trout from his line and he hit it with a tree branch: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Sullivan)
- Same strikes and dates; Guinness record: [The Weather Network](https://www.theweathernetwork.com/en/news/weather/severe/this-day-in-weather-history-june-25-1977-human-lightning), [IrishCentral](https://www.irishcentral.com/opinion/others/roy-sullivan-struck-lightning-seven-times)
- Wikipedia notes the strikes were recorded by the park superintendent, who was often not there to see them.

## koepcke-jungle-fall-luck
- Juliane Koepcke (born 10 October 1954, so 17), LANSA Flight 508, 24 December 1971, flying with her mother over the Peruvian rainforest; the plane was struck by lightning and broke up in the air; she fell about 3,000 m still strapped to her row of seats; broken collarbone, a deep cut on her right arm, an eye injury, a knee ligament torn, concussion; followed a creek to a river; after nine days found a lumberjacks' camp, and the returning lumberjacks found her; 11 days in the jungle; the sole survivor; later a bat scientist (mammalogist) who runs the Panguana research station in the Peruvian rainforest: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juliane_Koepcke), [LANSA Flight 508](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LANSA_Flight_508)
- Fall and rescue: [HowStuffWorks](https://history.howstuffworks.com/historical-figures/juliane-koepcke.htm), [Snopes](https://www.snopes.com/fact-check/juliane-koepcke-plane-crash/)

## morgan-scratchie-luck
- Bill Morgan, an Australian truck driver; after a 1998 car crash he was given a drug he was allergic to, had a heart attack and was clinically dead for about 14 minutes (14 minutes 38 seconds in some reports) before being revived; he was in a coma for 12 days and his family was advised to turn off life support; he woke and recovered; about a year later he won a car (about A$27,000) on a scratch card; Melbourne's Nine News filmed a story and asked him to re-enact the purchase; he bought another card on camera and won A$250,000: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Morgan_lottery_win), [LotteryUSA](https://www.lotteryusa.com/news/the-unbelievable-story-bill-morgan), [Now I Know](https://nowiknow.com/?p=5417)

## alvarenga-adrift-luck
- José Salvador Alvarenga, a Salvadoran fisherman working in Mexico; he set out from Chiapas with a young crewmate (Ezequiel Córdoba) on 17 November 2012; a storm disabled their boat's motor and they drifted out into the Pacific; he caught fish, turtles and birds with his bare hands and drank rainwater (and turtle blood or urine when there was no rain); Córdoba died weeks or months in; on 30 January 2014, after 438 days, he reached Ebon Atoll in the Marshall Islands, swimming ashore; he drifted an estimated 5,500-6,700 miles (about 10,800 km): [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Salvador_Alvarenga), [GeoGarage (The Guardian)](https://blog.geogarage.com/2015/11/lost-at-sea-man-who-vanished-for-14.html), [Billiken](https://billiken.lat/personas/jose-salvador-alvarenga-conoce-al-salvadoreno-que-sobrevivio-tras-438-dias-a-la-deriva-en-el-oceano-pacifico/)

## jessop-three-ships-luck
- Violet Jessop, Irish ocean liner stewardess and Voluntary Aid Detachment nurse. Aboard RMS Olympic on 20 September 1911 when it collided with the warship HMS Hawke. On the Titanic in 1912: ordered into lifeboat 16, and an officer gave her a baby to look after. On HMHS Britannic (a hospital ship) on 21 November 1916 when it struck a German naval mine; she jumped out of her lifeboat as the ship's propellers were shredding lifeboats, and survived a traumatic head injury. Returned to work for White Star Line in 1920, later Red Star Line and Royal Mail Line; retired 1950; died 1971 aged 83: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violet_Jessop)
- Same three ships and events: [History UK](https://history.co.uk/articles/unsinkable-violet-jessop), [Today I Found Out](https://www.todayifoundout.com/index.php/2014/01/woman-survived-sinking-titanic-britannic-collision-olympic/)

## yamaguchi-two-bombs-luck
- Tsutomu Yamaguchi, Mitsubishi Heavy Industries engineer, on a business trip to Hiroshima; on 6 August 1945 he was preparing to leave the city and was walking towards the docks at 8:15; serious burns on the left side of his upper body, eardrums ruptured; went home to Nagasaki; went to work bandaged on 9 August and was telling his supervisor about the Hiroshima blast when the Nagasaki bomb flashed; died 4 January 2010, aged 93: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsutomu_Yamaguchi) (cites The Guardian, The Independent, The Times)
- "Suddenly the same white light filled the room" (his words, 2009 interview with The Independent), in the office in Nagasaki telling his boss about Hiroshima: [The Awl](https://www.theawl.com/?p=27275)
- Burns to his upper body, spent the night in Hiroshima, returned to Nagasaki, the second bomb three days later; recognised by Japan in March 2009 as a survivor of both; died of stomach cancer at 93: [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna34722073), [Hiroshima Peace Media Center](https://www.hiroshimapeacemedia.jp/?p=14868)

- Thirty metres is about a ten-storey building (about 3 m a storey): rough comparison, said as "picture".
- Betty Lou Oliver ran an elevator ("elevator operator"), age 20, B-25 in fog, cables cut, fall about 75 storeys: Oliver sources above.
