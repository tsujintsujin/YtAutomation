## Channel description

Why does your cat shove the pen off the desk while staring right at you? Why does a dog turn three circles before it finally flops down? Pets do a lot of weird stuff, and most of it has a reason. Sometimes a really old one.

That's what this channel is about. Pet behavior, the why behind it, and the history of how our animals ended up doing it in the first place. One habit per video, followed all the way back: where it started, what the actual research says, and what it means for the little creature asleep on your couch right now.

You'll see a lot of a Geek and his silver tabby. The cat follows him into the bathroom. He's made his peace with it, mostly.

If you've ever looked at your pet and just thought "...why?", you're in the right place.

## Video 1

### Title
Why Do Cats Follow You Everywhere?

### Description
You get up for a glass of water. Soft thump behind you. Yep, the cat's up too. It isn't hungry and it doesn't want to play, it just has to be wherever you are, apparently. So how does an animal with a reputation for not caring end up trailing you around like a small furry shadow?

Turns out it starts about ten thousand years ago, with grain stores, a lot of mice and some wildcats that more or less tamed themselves. From there: kitten habits that never switched off, a 2019 study where cats bonded with their owners about as often as human babies do, cats picking a person over food (food came second!), the mental map your cat keeps of where you are in the house, territory and why closed doors drive them nuts, and the boring practical stuff too, like food, routine, warmth and plain boredom. And yes, the bathroom thing. Plus when a cat that suddenly won't leave your side might be telling you something's wrong.

## Video 2

### Title
Why Does Your Cat Knead You?

### Description
Long day. You finally flop onto the couch and, what, three seconds later? The cat's on your lap. One turn, then another, and the paws get going. Left, right, left, right, purring like a tiny outboard motor... and then a claw goes clean through your jeans. The internet calls this making biscuits. Cute name. Still doesn't explain why a grown predator wants to knead you like bread dough.

It goes back to day one, honestly. A newborn kitten pushes on its mother's belly to get the milk flowing, and the habit just never switches off. We get into that, plus why some cats drool or suck on blankets mid-knead, the scent glands in their paws (yep, it's marking you as its own), the old idea that wild cats flattened grass to make a bed, the stretching and self-soothing side of it, why some cats never knead at all, air biscuits, lions and tigers doing it too. And the practical bit: handling the claws without punishing your cat, why declawing is never the answer, and when kneading is worth a word with your vet.
