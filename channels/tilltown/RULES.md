# Tilltown: rules

How a Tilltown video is chosen, written, checked, made and posted. Built from `research/04-script.md` (9 videos read in
full) and `research/05-packaging.md`; changed only from our own numbers (`LEARNINGS.md`), with the date and reason.

## 1. The topic
A topic goes ahead only if it has **all four**:
1. **A big concrete number** (a fortune, a loss, a price, a count).
2. **A reversal or paradox** ("the company did it to itself", "he got rich again", "the debt was zero once").
3. **Conflict between people** (founder vs board, con man vs mark, regulator vs company).
4. **A stake for the viewer** (your savings, your prices, the scam that still works today).

And **a known subject or a giant number** people already search for. The first 5 videos: famous, searched subjects
only (a floor of search viewers for a brand-new channel).
Before writing: search YouTube for the subject. If a big channel covered it in the last few months, find a different
angle or drop it. Never repeat our own premise.

## 2. Title and thumbnail come first
- **Title:** the known subject or the giant number + one gap. Under ~60 characters, plain words, a question or a
  "what happened". Shapes that hit: "What Really Happened to X?", "X Explained Simply", "Who Does X Owe $40 Trillion?",
  "The $X Company You've Never Heard Of". Never promise what the video doesn't pay off.
- **Thumbnail:** one face (a caricature of the real person) or a Tilltown character with a strong expression, one
  object, 1-4 huge words that add to the title (never repeat it), high contrast. Check it at 160 px wide.
- No strong pair = no video.

## 3. The script (12-16 min, ~2,300-2,900 words; our voice reads ~187 words a minute)
| Part | Time | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Cold open | 0:00-0:30 | The most extreme concrete fact, made human-sized; the twist; why it touches the viewer. Shows the thumbnail's thing in the first 5 s. |
| Promise | 0:30-1:00 | The one central question (or 3); "by the end you'll know..." |
| Subscribe line | ~1:00 | One line with a reason ("this channel breaks down how money really works behind..."). |
| Sections | 1:00 on | Each answers one smaller question and **ends by asking the next one**. |
| The turn | ~40-60% | One decision, a conflict, a betrayal or a crash. |
| Low point | | The moment it looks lost; a character asks what the viewer is thinking. |
| Consequences / comeback | | What happened next, in exact numbers and dates. |
| Payoff | last 2-3 min | Every promised question answered plainly; something the viewer can use. |
| Close | last 20-30 s | Callback to the opening object or person; one closing line; the next video, set up by a thread from this one. |

Inside every section:
- Something new every 20-40 s: a number, a reveal, a line of dialogue, a joke, a scene change. No exposition block over
  ~40 s.
- **Explain mechanisms in Tilltown** with its recurring cast, then give the real numbers.
- Short dialogue lines (one or two sentences) from characters; humour welcome.
- Show the number, then shrink it ("$40 trillion = $118,000 for every American").
- Real people, real quotes (under 15 words), exact dates, named sources in the narration ("according to Reuters").
- Give the other side, then the evidence. **Opinion is said as opinion** ("our view, not a fact").
- Callbacks: an object or character from the open comes back at the end.

Wording: written for the ear; short sentences; plain words first, the real term once; "but" and "so", not "and then";
questions to the viewer, then the answer; "you" in every explanation; concrete over adjectives; numbers rounded for the
ear unless the exactness is the point.

## 4. Facts, copyright, legal (before any voice is made)
- **Fact check:** every number, date, name and claim in `script.md` has a line in `sources.md` (claim → source URL →
  what the source says). A claim without a source is cut or rewritten.
- **Real people and companies:** say only what a named source or court record says. Allegations are called
  allegations, with who made them. No guessing at motives as fact.
- **Copyright:** only our own animation and our own Gemini drawings. No film, TV or news clips, no logos used as
  pictures, no photos. Music only from YouTube's Audio Library. Quotes stay short.
- "Not financial advice" in every description; no telling viewers what to buy.

## 5. Shorts (30-50 s)
- The single most jaw-dropping fact of a long video, **complete on its own**: the fact as the first line (no intro),
  why it's true in 2-3 beats, a twist, a last line that points to the long video or loops to the first line.
- Movement in the very first frame. Never a trailer that stops mid-story.
- Made from the long video's scenes, re-laid out at 9:16. "Related video" = its long video.

## 6. Look and sound (fixed after the phase-1 test)
- Scenes: HyperFrames animation (Tilltown, its cast, charts, counters, maps, timelines, documents). Gemini only for
  caricatures of real people, big dramatic moments and thumbnail art (~20-40 per video).
- Captions: 2-4 words at a time, bold, one key word coloured.
- Voice: Chatterbox (pending Justin's choice, `HANDOFF.md` "Waiting on Justin" #3).
- Every scene's design is its own; reusing Tilltown and the cast is fine, reusing whole scenes is not.

## 7. Posting
- Long video: title, description (humanized, chapters, sources summary, "not financial advice"), tags, 3 thumbnails
  in Test & Compare, `.srt` subtitles, playlist, "Made for kids: No", category Education, "Altered content" per the
  voice decision. Private until Justin has watched video 1; after that Claude publishes.
- Shorts: posted on the days between long videos; each with "Related video" = its long video.
- Check every upload in Studio after posting (visibility, thumbnail, subtitles, Related video).
