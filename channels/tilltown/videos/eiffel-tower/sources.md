# Sources: The Man Who Sold the Eiffel Tower

Checked 2026-11-07. Each line: the claim in `script.md` → the source → what the source says.

| Claim | Source | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| In 1925, a man in Paris (Lustig) | [S] | Lustig arrived in Paris in May 1925 (per the memoir of Secret Service agent James Johnson) |
| He sold the Eiffel Tower to a scrap-metal dealer named André Poisson | [E] | "Scrap dealer André Poisson took the bait"; he paid before realising he'd been duped |
| All 7,300 tons of iron | [E] | He thought he had bought "the 7,300 tons of iron from the Tower" |
| The tower was never for sale | [E] | The plan was "far from true"; the Tower's future had been secured since the 1910s |
| Poisson didn't go to the police; too embarrassed | [E] | "so embarrassed that he didn't press charges" |
| The con man left the country | [E] | Lustig "had fled to Austria" |
| Later, he came back to try it again | [E] [M] | [E]: "Some time later, Lustig tried to sell the Tower again"; [M]: he returned to Paris |
| In 2025, people reported losing about $920 million to government impersonators | [F1] | "about $920 million to government impersonators" in 2025 (reports to the FTC) |
| The same three tricks: look official / a reason not to check / money you can't chase | [F2] [S] [E] [M] | [F2]: impostors give an "employee ID number" to sound official, give a reason to pay immediately, insist on payment that is hard to get back. Lustig: government seal and grand hotel [S]; "political problems I cannot discuss" [S]; the victim too ashamed to go to the police [E] (the payment called a bribe in [M]) |
| He read in the papers the tower cost a fortune to keep up | [E] | read in the press about "expensive maintenance and declining attendance" |
| He had letters printed with the French government's seal | [S] | "commissioned stationary carrying the official French government seal" |
| Hôtel de Crillon, on the Place de la Concorde | [S] | "a stone palace on the Place de la Concorde" |
| Wrote to the top men in France's scrap-metal trade, inviting them to a meeting | [S] | wrote "to the top people in the French scrap metal industry, inviting them to the hotel" |
| In a quiet room: costly repairs, political problems he could not discuss | [S] | reportedly said: "engineering faults, costly repairs, and political problems I cannot discuss" |
| It would go to the highest bidder | [S] | "The tower would be sold to the highest bidder" |
| Tilltown scene (the letter, the clock tower) | — | Our illustration, not a fact |

**Kept out (sources disagree):** the price Poisson paid ([E]: "an unknown sum"; others give 70,000 or 1.2M francs);
