## Cold open
In 1925, a man in Paris sold the Eiffel Tower.
All seven thousand three hundred tons of iron.
The buyer was a scrap-metal dealer named André Poisson.
The tower was never for sale.
And when Poisson found out, he didn't go to the police.
He was too embarrassed.
So the con man left the country... and later, he came back to try it again.

## Promise
How do you sell a landmark you don't own, to a man who buys scrap for a living?
It took three tricks.
And here's the part that should worry you.
In 2025, people told the FTC they lost about nine hundred and twenty million dollars to scammers pretending to be the government.
The same three tricks.
By the end of this video, you'll spot all three.

## Trick one: look official
Picture it in Tilltown.
A letter lands on the junk dealer's counter.
Heavy paper. The mayor's own seal.
"The old clock tower is coming down. You have been chosen to bid."
Would you argue with the mayor's seal?
That's exactly what Victor Lustig built.
He read in the papers that the Eiffel Tower cost a fortune to keep up.
He had letters printed with the French government's seal.
He set himself up at the Hôtel de Crillon, a palace of a hotel on the Place de la Concorde.
And he wrote to the top men in France's scrap-metal trade, inviting them to a meeting.
In a quiet room, he told them the tower had to come down: costly repairs, and political problems he could not discuss.
It would go to the highest bidder.
