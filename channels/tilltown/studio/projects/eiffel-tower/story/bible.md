# Bible: "The Man Who Sold the Eiffel Tower" (opening, 85 s)
Logline: in 1925 con man Victor Lustig sold the Eiffel Tower to scrap dealer André Poisson; the same three tricks still
take $920 million a year from people today. The narrator speaks to "you" (the viewer, shown as YOU in the 2025 shots).
Tone: true-crime suspense with a wink (Lustig's smirk); Tilltown beats are warm and a little playful.
Style: `tilltown` (indigo nights, amber gaslight rims, gold money accents). Captions off. Bars: wide; scope on freezes
and the big close-ups.

## Motifs
- **The three gold cards** numbered 1-3 = the three tricks (s012, s016, s017); later in the video each card lights
  when its trick is named.
- **The seal**: the French government seal (red wax and gold) and the Tilltown mayor's seal are drawn the same way,
  so the viewer links them (s021, s024, s025, s027).
- **Gold** = money and the con's bait; **coral** = the loss and the warning (the $920 million, NOT FOR SALE).
- Lustig's smirk lands on the twist words ("sold", "never", "built").
