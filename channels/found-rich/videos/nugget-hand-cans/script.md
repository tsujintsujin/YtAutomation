# A rusty can in the dirt led to $10 million in gold

A couple saw a rusty can sticking out of the dirt. It led to gold coins worth about ten million dollars. But first, two lumps of gold that were hiding just under the ground.

In twenty thirteen, a man was out with a metal detector near Ballarat, in Australia. That town had a big gold rush more than a hundred and fifty years ago. People have searched those hills for ages. Then his detector beeped. About sixty centimetres down, he dug up a gold nugget. It weighed five and a half kilos. Experts said it was worth over three hundred thousand Australian dollars.

Way back in nineteen eighty, another man in Australia was out with his metal detector too. Near a small town called Kingower, it beeped. Just thirty centimetres down, a huge nugget was standing on its end. It weighed about twenty-seven kilos. It's still the biggest nugget ever found with a metal detector. A casino in Las Vegas bought it for over a million dollars. It's called the Hand of Faith, and it's still on show there.

Now, the cans. In twenty thirteen, a couple in California were walking their dog on their land. They spotted an old rusty can poking out of the ground. They pried it loose with a stick. It was heavy. Inside were gold coins. They went back with tools and found eight cans in all. More than fourteen hundred gold coins, from the eighteen hundreds. Lots of them looked brand new. Nobody knows who buried them. Experts said they could be worth about ten million dollars.

## Why it is built this way

- One theme: gold hiding just under the ground (two nuggets, then buried cans).
- Hook teases the biggest find (the cans); smallest to biggest: A$300,000 (valued), over $1 million (sold), about $10 million (estimated, so "could be worth").
- The 1980 nugget comes second although it is older: it is the bigger money. "Way back in" marks the jump in time.
- Nobody is named: the Ballarat finder and the California couple stayed anonymous.
