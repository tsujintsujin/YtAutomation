# A $14,000 egg was worth $33 million

A man paid fourteen thousand dollars for a golden egg. He planned to melt it down. Lucky for him, he didn't. But first, two other cheap finds that turned out to be worth a fortune.

A man in England was looking around a car boot sale. That's a market where people sell old stuff from the back of their cars. He spotted an old watch for twenty-five pounds. It turned out to be the watch James Bond wore in the film Thunderball. In twenty thirteen, it sold for over a hundred thousand pounds.

Then there's a couple who sell old clothes online. At a Goodwill bin store, they paid fifty-eight cents for an old sweater with West Point on the front. Later, while watching a show about football coach Vince Lombardi, they saw him wearing that same kind of sweater. They checked the tag. It said Lombardi. It sold for forty-three thousand dollars.

Now, the egg. A scrap metal dealer bought it at a market in the American Midwest. He wanted to melt it for the gold. But nobody would pay what he asked. So one night he searched online for the name on the little clock inside. The egg was made by Fabergé for the Russian royal family, back in the eighteen eighties. It had been lost for decades. Experts said it was worth about thirty-three million dollars.

Would you have melted it?

## Why it is built this way

- Same shape as the first Short: the hook teases the biggest find (the egg), two smaller finds come first, the egg is told last and the last line asks the viewer something.
- Smallest to biggest: about £100,000, then $43,000 for 58 cents (the biggest jump), then $33 million.
- Most lines are cut in two, so the picture changes every 1-2 seconds.
- "Car boot sale" is said in plain words right after it, because many viewers won't know it.
