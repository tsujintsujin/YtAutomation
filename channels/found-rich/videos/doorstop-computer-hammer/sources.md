# Sources

- Edmore meteorite: a 22.5 lb iron-nickel rock used as a doorstop by David Mazurek since buying an Edmore, Michigan farm in 1988; the seller said it fell in the 1930s; Central Michigan University identified it in 2018; valued at about $100,000 (no sale price reported, so the script says "valued at"): [Fox 17](https://fox17online.com/2018/10/04/sitting-on-a-fortune-man-uses-100000-meteorite-as-doorstop-for-decades/), [NBC](https://www.nbcwashington.com/news/weird/rock-used-as-doorstop-is-actually-a-meteorite-worth-100k/54666/)
- Apple I dropped off at Clean Bay Area (Milpitas, California) in 2015 by a woman clearing out her garage, who left no contact details; sold for $200,000; the company offered her half: [KTVU](https://www.ktvu.com/news/woman-tosses-apple-computer-that-turns-out-to-be-worth-200k), [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/100000-reward-waiting-woman-who-dumped-rare-apple-i-180955464/)
- Hoxne Hoard: November 1992, farmer Peter Whatling lost a hammer; his friend Eric Lawes searched with a metal detector and found silver spoons, gold jewellery and about 15,000 Roman coins, the largest late Roman hoard of gold and silver found in Britain; Lawes was paid £1.75 million and shared it with Whatling; the hammer was found too: [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/search-lost-hammer-led-largest-cache-roman-treasure-ever-found-britain-180967263/), [World History Encyclopedia](https://www.worldhistory.org/article/932/the-roman-hoxne-hoard/)
