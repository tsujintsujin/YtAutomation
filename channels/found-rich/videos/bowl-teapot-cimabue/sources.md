# Sources

- Yongle-period (Ming dynasty) blue-and-white bowl bought for $35 at a yard sale near New Haven, Connecticut in 2020; the buyer emailed photos to Sotheby's; one of only seven known (six companions, mostly in museums); sold at Sotheby's New York 17 March 2021 for $721,800: [Artnet](https://news.artnet.com/market/chinese-bowl-from-yard-sale-auction-1952881), [Antiques and the Arts](https://www.antiquesandthearts.com/connecticut-35-yard-sale-find-takes-721800-at-sothebys)
- John Bartlam teapot (Cain Hoy, South Carolina, 1760s), among the earliest American porcelain, broken; bought for £15 in 2016; sold at Woolley and Wallis 20 Feb 2018 for £575,000 including fees; went to the Metropolitan Museum of Art: [Yahoo / UK news](https://uk.news.yahoo.com/broken-teapot-bought-15-sells-103958135.html), [Artnet](https://news.artnet.com/market/met-buys-rare-american-teapot-1230278)
- Cimabue, The Mocking of Christ (about 1280): found in 2019 by auctioneer Philomène Wolf hanging above a hot plate in an elderly woman's kitchen in Compiègne, France, which she took for a religious icon; sold by Actéon 27 Oct 2019 for about €24 million: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mocking_of_Christ_(Cimabue)), [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/lost-renaissance-masterpiece-worth-6-million-found-hanging-above-womans-hotplate-180973215/)
