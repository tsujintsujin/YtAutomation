# A painting covering a hole was worth $1.25 million

A man hung a painting over a hole in his wall. It was worth one point two five million dollars. But first, two other fortunes that were hiding inside people's homes.

In Minnesota, a man bought an old house for about ten thousand dollars, to fix it up. When he tore open a wall, he found old newspapers stuffed inside to keep the heat in. In the middle of them was a comic book from nineteen thirty-eight. It was the very first Superman comic. The back cover got torn soon after, and that cost him a lot. It still sold for a hundred seventy-five thousand dollars.

Then a couple in England were fixing their kitchen floor. Under the concrete, they found a small pot. It was full of gold coins, more than two hundred sixty of them, hidden about three hundred years ago. In twenty twenty-two they sold at auction for seven hundred fifty-four thousand pounds.

Now, the painting. A man in Indiana had an old painting of flowers. He'd hung it on his wall just to hide a hole. One day he was playing a board game called Masterpiece. One of the game's paintings looked a lot like his. It turned out his was by the same artist, Martin Johnson Heade. A museum in Houston bought it for one point two five million dollars.

What's hiding in your walls?

## Why it is built this way

- One theme: all three were hidden inside a home (a wall, a floor, a painting over a hole).
- Hook teases the biggest find (the painting); smallest to biggest: $175,000, £754,000, $1.25 million.
- The torn cover is a small twist in the middle of the comic story, so it isn't just "found it, sold it".
- No comic character is drawn: the comic cover is only shapes.
