# A vase in a shoebox sold for €16 million

A family brought a vase to an auction house in a shoebox. It sold for sixteen million euros. But first, two other things people forgot they had.

Back in the nineteen eighties, someone bought a Super Mario Brothers game as a Christmas present. It never got opened. It went into a desk drawer and stayed there for about thirty-five years. In twenty twenty-one, still sealed in its plastic, it sold for six hundred sixty thousand dollars.

Then an antiques dealer in Scotland bought a small carved chess piece in nineteen sixty-four. He paid five pounds. His family kept it in a drawer for more than fifty years. Then experts looked at it. It was one of the Lewis chessmen, a famous set carved about eight hundred years ago. In twenty nineteen it sold for seven hundred thirty-five thousand pounds.

Now, the vase. A family in France found it up in their attic, with other things they'd inherited. They wrapped it in newspaper, put it in a shoebox, and took it to Sotheby's in Paris. It was a Chinese vase made for an emperor in the seventeen hundreds. The experts guessed it might sell for about half a million euros. It sold for sixteen million.

So, have you checked your attic?

## Why it is built this way

- One theme: things people forgot they owned (a drawer, a drawer, an attic).
- Hook teases the biggest find (the vase) with the strange detail (a shoebox); smallest to biggest: $660,000, £735,000, €16 million.
- The vase ends on the gap between the guess (half a million) and the result (sixteen million): the payoff.
- "Super Mario Brothers" is written out so the voice says it right. No game character is drawn.
