# A "spoon" in a field led to a £2 million treasure

A man with a metal detector dug up a spoon. At least, he thought it was a spoon. It led to a treasure worth almost two million pounds. But first, two more groups of friends who got very lucky.

In England, two friends went metal detecting on a farm in Staffordshire. Just under the ground, they found three gold rings, big enough to wear around your neck. Weeks later, they found a gold bracelet too. They were over two thousand years old, the oldest gold jewellery of their kind ever found in Britain. A museum paid three hundred twenty-five thousand pounds for them. The friends and the farmer shared the money.

Then in Yemen, thirty-five fishermen were out at sea. They spotted a dead whale floating. It had a strong, nasty smell, and they knew what that might mean. They brought it to the beach and cut it open. Inside was a big lump of ambergris. It's a waxy stuff whales make in their gut, and it helps perfume last longer. They sold it for one and a half million dollars. They split it equally, and gave some to poor families in their town.

Now, the spoon. In two thousand fourteen, Derek McLennan was detecting on church land in Scotland. Two ministers were with him. Then he dug up a piece of silver. He thought it was a spoon. It was a silver arm band from Viking times. They found a silver cross, and over a hundred more pieces of gold, silver and glass. A museum in Edinburgh paid almost two million pounds for it all. He agreed to share the money with the church.

## Why it is built this way

- One theme: friends out together (two detecting friends, a fishing crew, a detectorist with two church ministers) who all shared the money.
- Hook teases the biggest find (the "spoon"); smallest to biggest: £325,000, $1.5 million, £1.98 million.
- "Ambergris" is said once and explained in plain words right after it (rule: say what a hard word means).
- "From Viking times": experts are not sure whether Vikings or Anglo-Saxons buried it, so the script never says Vikings buried it.
- Derek McLennan is named: he spoke to the press many times. The two ministers and the torc finders are not named.
