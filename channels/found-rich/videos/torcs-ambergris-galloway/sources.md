# Sources

- Leekfrith torcs: found by friends Mark Hambleton and Joe Kania with metal detectors on 11 December 2016 in a field at Leekfrith, Staffordshire, with the landowner's permission; three gold neck torcs 15-20 cm below the surface, a fourth piece (a bracelet) weeks later; made about 400-250 BC, the oldest Iron Age gold jewellery found in Britain; valued at £325,000 by the Treasure Valuation Committee and bought by the Potteries Museum & Art Gallery; the money shared between the finders and the landowner: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leekfrith_torcs), [National Heritage Memorial Fund](https://nhmf.org.uk/news/earliest-iron-age-gold-ever-discovered-britain-saved), [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/world/metal-detector-hobbyists-discover-oldest-iron-age-jewelry-ever-found-n726646)
- Yemen ambergris: 35 fishermen in the Gulf of Aden found a floating sperm whale carcass in early 2021, suspected ambergris from its smell, cut it open on shore and found about 127 kg (280 lb); sold to a buyer in the UAE for about $1.5 million; split equally, with part given to poor families in their community: [Soundings](https://soundingsonline.com/news/fishing-crew-finds-rare-whale-vomit-worth-1-5-million/), [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/fishermen-1-5m-whale-vomit-lifted-out-poverty-yemen-1596801?amp=1)
- Galloway Hoard: found in September 2014 by Derek McLennan on Church of Scotland glebe land near Balmaghie, Kirkcudbrightshire, with two ministers (Rev Dr David Bartholomew and Pastor Mike Smith) who also detect; the first piece, a silver arm ring, he took for a spoon; then a silver cross and more than 100 gold, silver, glass, crystal and other objects from the Viking Age (about 900 AD); bought by National Museums Scotland (Edinburgh) for £1.98 million; McLennan and the Church agreed to share the proceeds: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galloway_Hoard), [ITV](https://www.itv.com/news/border/story/2014-10-13/viking-hoard-discovered/)
