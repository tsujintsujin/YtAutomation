# A car under old magazines sold for €16 million

A car sat under piles of old magazines for fifty years. Then it sold for over sixteen million euros. But first, two other things that people left behind and forgot about.

A man who hunts for old jeans climbed down into an abandoned mine in the American West. In the dirt, he found a pair of Levi's from the eighteen eighties. They were worn, but they were still in one piece. Only a couple of pairs like them are known. In twenty twenty-two, they sold at an auction for over eighty-seven thousand dollars. Not bad for a dirty pair of pants.

Then in Ohio, a family was clearing out their grandfather's old house. In the attic, they found an old box. Inside were about seven hundred baseball cards from around nineteen ten. Back then, you got them free with candy. Their grandfather probably handed them out at his meat market. The cards looked almost new. Just thirty-seven of them sold for over five hundred sixty thousand dollars.

Now, the car. In France, a man named Roger Baillon collected fancy cars. He dreamed of opening a museum. Then the money ran out. His cars were left in old barns and sheds. In twenty fourteen, his family opened the doors. Inside were about sixty old cars, all covered in dust. Under stacks of old magazines sat a Ferrari from nineteen sixty-one. A French movie star had once owned it. In twenty fifteen, it sold at an auction in Paris for over sixteen million euros.

What's been sitting in your garage all these years?

## Why it is built this way

- One theme: all three were left behind and forgotten for decades (a mine, an attic, a barn).
- Hook teases the biggest find (the Ferrari); smallest to biggest: $87,400, $566,132, €16.3 million.
- The jeans hunter is not named: the reports don't say for sure who sold them at the auction.
- "Probably handed them out": the family believes this, it isn't proven.
- The movie star is not named or drawn as anyone real: a stick figure on a film set.
