# A $3 bowl sold for $2.2 million

A family paid three dollars for a little bowl at a yard sale. Years later, it sold for over two million. But first, two other bargains that paid off big.

A man in Arizona went into a Goodwill store. He was looking for something else, but a case of old watches caught his eye. One of them cost five dollars and ninety-nine cents. He knew the name on the dial. It was a rare diving watch from the nineteen fifties, with a built-in alarm. He sold it for thirty-five thousand dollars.

Then a student in Berlin bought a used sofa bed at a flea market, for two hundred fifteen dollars. When the student pulled it out to make the bed, a small painting was tucked inside. It was about four hundred years old. It sold at an auction for over twenty-seven thousand dollars.

Now, the bowl. In two thousand seven, a family in New York bought it at a yard sale. It was small and plain, and creamy white. They kept it on their mantel. Then they asked experts at Sotheby's to look at it. It was about a thousand years old, made in China. Only one other like it is known, and it's in a museum in London. In twenty thirteen, the bowl sold for two point two million dollars.

What's sitting on your shelf right now?

## Why it is built this way

- Hook teases the biggest find (the bowl); two smaller finds first; the bowl last; the last line asks the viewer something.
- Smallest to biggest: $27,000, $35,000, then $2.2 million. The watch is told first because it is the simpler story.
- The Berlin student is "the student" (no he/she): the reports differ.
