# A field eight people searched hid £3 million in gold

Eight people had already searched a farm field and found nothing. Then one more man tried, and found gold worth over three million pounds. But first, two finds that didn't look like much at all.

In Ohio, a man bought an old poster at a thrift store for fourteen dollars and fourteen cents. He thought it was a cheap copy. It was an ad for a Picasso show from nineteen fifty-eight. Then he spotted a signature in red. Experts looked at it. It wasn't a copy. It was a real print, made by Picasso himself. He sold it for seven thousand dollars.

Then in England, a man with a metal detector was searching a farm in Kent. He dug up a small gold cup. A plough had crushed it. Still, it was about three and a half thousand years old. Only a handful of cups like it have ever been found in Europe. The British Museum bought it for two hundred seventy thousand pounds. The finder and the farm's owners split the money.

Now, the field. In two thousand nine, a man named Terry Herbert took his metal detector to a farm near Lichfield. Eight others had searched that field before him. Then his detector beeped. Gold came up out of the dirt. He kept digging for five more days. He found more than fifteen hundred pieces of gold and silver. Lots of them came from old swords. They were about thirteen hundred years old. Two museums paid over three point two million pounds for it all. Terry and the farmer split it down the middle.

## Why it is built this way

- One theme: none of the three looked like much (a "cheap copy", a crushed cup, a field already searched).
- Hook teases the biggest find (the field); smallest to biggest: $7,000, £270,000, £3.285 million.
- "A real print, made by Picasso himself": it is a linocut he made, not a painting; the narration never says painting.
- Terry Herbert is named: he spoke to the press many times. The farmer is not named.
