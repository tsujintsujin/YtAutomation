# Sources

- Picasso linocut: Zach Bodish paid $14.14 at a Volunteers of America thrift store in Columbus, Ohio, for what he thought was a reproduction poster; it was a linocut Picasso made to advertise his 1958 Easter exhibition of ceramics in Vallauris, signed in red; sold privately in April 2012 for $7,000: [Bangor Daily News / AP](https://www.bangordailynews.com/2012/05/10/news/ohio-mans-luck-changes-with-signed-picasso-print/), [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/lifestyle/rare-picasso-found-at-ohio-thrift-store.amp)
- Ringlemere Cup: found by Cliff Bradshaw with a metal detector on 4 November 2001 at Ringlemere Farm near Sandwich, Kent; crushed by plough damage; Bronze Age, about 1700-1500 BC; only seven comparable gold cups known from Europe; bought by the British Museum for £270,000, split between Bradshaw and the farm's owners (the Smith family): [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ringlemere_Cup), [Kent HER](https://heritage.kent.gov.uk/Monument/MKE41636)
- Staffordshire Hoard: Terry Herbert found it with a metal detector on 5 July 2009 in a ploughed field near Lichfield owned by Fred Johnson, where eight other detectorists had found nothing; he found more over the next five days; more than 1,500 gold and silver pieces (later counts over 3,000 fragments), mostly fittings from swords, Anglo-Saxon (7th century); valued at £3.285 million, bought by Birmingham Museum and the Potteries Museum, the money split equally between finder and landowner: [Medievalists.net](https://www.medievalists.net/2009/11/how-the-staffordshire-hoard-was-discovered/), [RESCUE](https://rescue-archaeology.org.uk/2010/01/25/staffordshire-hoard-worth-3-285-million/)
