# Sources

- Vale of York Hoard: found in January 2007 near Harrogate by father and son David and Andrew Whelan, metal detecting as a hobby; a gilded silver cup holding 617 coins, 67 silver objects (arm rings, ingots, chopped silver) and one gold arm ring, hidden in the Viking Age (about 927-928 AD); bought jointly by the British Museum and York Museums Trust for £1,082,800 in 2009: [York Museums Trust](https://www.yorkmuseumstrust.org.uk/blog/beyond-jorvik-the-vale-of-york-viking-hoard-andrew-woods/), [History News Network](https://www.historynewsnetwork.org/article/116117), [Paul Fraser Collectibles](https://paulfrasercollectibles.com/blogs/unique-items/metal-detector-finds-1-1m)
- Crosby Garrett Helmet: found in May 2010 by an unnamed detectorist in his twenties, detecting with his father, on a farm at Crosby Garrett, Cumbria; in 33 large and 34 small fragments, restored; a Roman cavalry parade helmet with a face-mask visor, late 2nd or early 3rd century AD; not treasure under the Treasure Act (bronze only), so it could be sold; Christie's estimate £200,000-300,000; sold on 7 October 2010 for about £2.3 million: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crosby_Garrett_Helmet), [Yorkshire Post](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/news/roman-helmet-found-field-sold-ps23m-1955878)
- Chew Valley Hoard: found on 26 January 2019 near Bath by detectorists Lisa Grace and Adam Staples while teaching five friends to use metal detectors; 2,528 silver pennies from just after the Norman Conquest (1066), 1,236 of Harold II and 1,310 of William I; valued at £4.3 million in October 2024 and bought by the South West Heritage Trust; half to the landowner, the other half split equally between the two finders and their five friends: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chew_Valley_hoard), [ITV](https://www.itv.com/news/central/2019-08-28/couple-find-treasure-hoard-dating-back-1-000-years-and-worth-5m-with-metal-detector/), [Artnet](https://news.artnet.com/art-world/chew-valley-coin-hoard-most-valuable-2557886)
