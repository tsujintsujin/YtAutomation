# Sources

- Barber-surgeons cut hair, pulled teeth and did surgery, including bloodletting, from the Middle Ages on; the barber pole stands for the staff the patient gripped to make the blood flow; red and white poles for barbers (England); "red for blood, white for bandages" is a common explanation, not proven (so the script says "probably"): [Wikipedia: Barber's pole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barber%27s_pole), [Wikipedia: Barber surgeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barber_surgeon)
- Bloodletting: based on the four humours (blood, phlegm, black bile, yellow bile), sickness from too much blood; usually done by barber-surgeons; a vein in the forearm opened with a lancet, a tourniquet on the arm, blood caught in a bowl; amount by guesswork; it caused blood loss, anemia, infection and shock; declined in the eighteen hundreds; today only for a few conditions (e.g. hemochromatosis), and "phlebotomy" mostly means drawing small amounts for tests: [Wikipedia: Bloodletting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloodletting)
- Today's blood test: tourniquet on the upper arm, site cleaned with an alcohol swab, needle into the vein at a shallow angle, blood drawn into tubes, tourniquet released, needle out, pressure with gauze/cotton, plaster; tubes sent to the lab: [Geeky Medics: Venepuncture](https://geekymedics.com/venepuncture/), [Nurse.org: How to draw blood](https://nurse.org/articles/how-nurses-professionally-draw-blood)
