# Sources

- Knocker-ups: paid to wake people for work during the Industrial Revolution in Britain, Ireland and elsewhere, when alarm clocks were neither cheap nor reliable; a long light stick, often bamboo, for upstairs windows; clients paid a few pence a week; some would not leave until sure the client was awake; Mary Smith in East London (photographed 1931) shot dried peas through a long tube; in Ferryhill, County Durham, miners chalked their shift times on wall-mounted slates; the job lasted in places into the 1970s: [Wikipedia: Knocker-up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knocker-up)
- Phone timekeeping: a quartz crystal vibrates at 32,768 times a second when driven electrically; the real-time clock counts the pulses down to one per second and keeps counting while the phone sleeps; phones resync the time over the network: [Analog Devices: Clock timing](https://www.analog.com/en/resources/glossary/clock-timing.html), [Taitien: kHz crystals](https://www.taitien.com/feature/khz-series), [USC Scalar: Real time clocks](https://scalar.usc.edu/works/s-blogs-3/clocktiming---real-time-clocks-a-complete-guide)
- The alarm firing: the clock raises a wake-up at the set time while the rest of the phone sleeps (general knowledge of how phone alarms work, said plainly in the script).
- Taken from (structure only): "How We Woke Up Before Alarm Clocks" (Zack D. Films). Our own details (pay, pea shooter, miners' slates) and the now part are ours.
