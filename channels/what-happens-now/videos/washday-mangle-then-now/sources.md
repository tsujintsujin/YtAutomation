# Sources

- Wash day around 1900: Monday, a whole-day job starting early by lighting the copper; the copper filled by bucket from one cold tap; whites boiled with carbolic soap and soda; coloureds soaked in a wooden tub, scrubbed up and down on a washboard or agitated with a wooden dolly/posser; every item put through the mangle (rollers turned by a handle) to squeeze out the water; rinsed in cold water, through the mangle again; dried on the line, weather permitting: [1900s.org.uk: Washdays](https://www.1900s.org.uk/1900s-washdays.htm), [Birmingham History forum: washday, copper boilers, mangles](https://birminghamhistory.co.uk/forum/threads/washday-copper-boilers-mangles.33918), [Dundee: 1950s Monday washdays](https://bygone.dundeecity.gov.uk/reminiscences/1950s-monday-washdays/index.html)
- Today's washing machine: inlet valves fill the tub, the motor turns the drum and paddles lift and drop the clothes, the pump drains the dirty water, rinse with fresh water, then a fast spin (front loaders about 800 to 1400 turns a minute) flings the water out through the drum's holes: [Sears Home Services: How does a washer work](https://www.searshomeservices.com/blog/how-does-a-washer-work), [Croma: How does a washing machine work](https://www.croma.com/unboxed/how-does-a-washing-machine-work)
