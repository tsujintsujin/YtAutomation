# Washing clothes took a whole day

Around nineteen hundred, laundry wasn't a chore. It was a whole day, every Monday.

Before breakfast, you lit a fire under a big metal pot called a copper. You filled it bucket by bucket, from one cold tap. The whites went in to boil, with hard soap and soda. The colored clothes soaked in a wooden tub. Then you scrubbed them up and down on a washboard. Or you pounded them with a wooden plunger. Next, every single piece went through the mangle. You turned a big handle, and two rollers squeezed the dirty water out. Then you rinsed it all in cold water. And put it through the mangle again. Finally, you pegged everything on the line, and hoped it didn't rain.

Today, you throw it all in a machine. Add soap, and press a button. Water pours in by itself. The drum turns, lifting and dropping your clothes in soapy water. The dirty water is pumped out, and clean water rinses them. Then the drum spins up to fourteen hundred times a minute, and flings the water out through tiny holes. An hour or two later, it beeps. Done.

Back then, a whole Monday and sore hands. Now? One button.

## Why it is built this way

- Template (RULES.md): hook on the surprise (laundry took a whole day), the wash day step by step with "you" in
  the order it was done (copper, boil, soak, scrub, mangle, rinse, mangle, line), the machine step by step, one
  closing line.
- Our own topic (not in the reference Shorts), facts from first-hand accounts of wash days around 1900.
- The coloured thing follows the clothes and tools: the copper, the washboard, the mangle, the drum.
