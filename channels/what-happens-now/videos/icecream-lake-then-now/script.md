# Ice cream started on a frozen lake

In the eighteen hundreds, ice cream started on a frozen lake. In the middle of winter.

Men sawed the lake ice into big square blocks. Horses pulled the blocks home on sleds. The blocks went into an ice house, buried deep in sawdust. Sawdust kept the heat out, so the ice lasted into summer.

On a hot day, you chipped off a chunk and smashed it into little pieces. You poured cream, sugar and vanilla into a metal can. The can went into a wooden bucket. You packed crushed ice all around it, with handfuls of salt. Salt makes the ice even colder. Then you turned the crank. Inside, a paddle scraped the cream off the cold metal walls. You turned and turned until your arm ached. Slowly, the cream got thick. Then it froze.

Today, it's made in a factory. Milk, cream and sugar are mixed in giant steel tanks. The mix is heated to kill any germs, then cooled and left to rest for a few hours. A machine freezes it inside a cold steel pipe and whips in air, so it's soft. Bits of chocolate or fruit are added. Machines fill the tubs, and a tunnel of freezing air makes them hard. A freezer truck takes them to the store.

Back then, a frozen lake and an aching arm. Now? You just open the freezer.

## Why it is built this way

- Template (RULES.md): hook mid-process (the frozen lake), the before step by step with "you", the now step by
  step, one closing line with both side by side.
- Our own angle: the reference Short starts at the ice; ours follows the ice from the lake all the way to the bowl,
  and adds the crank freezer and the factory.
- The coloured thing is the ice (pale blue) wherever the ice is the subject, so the viewer follows it.
- The last two lines mirror each other: the aching arm at the crank, then a hand opening a home freezer.
