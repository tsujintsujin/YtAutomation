# Sources

- Ice cut from frozen lakes and ponds in winter, scored and sawn into blocks, floated to shore, hauled by horses; stored in ice houses packed in sawdust so it lasted into summer: [Wikipedia: Ice trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_trade), [Farm Collector: Ice cold cash crop](https://www.farmcollector.com/community/ice-cold-cash-crop-zm0z21sepzbut/), [Frontenac News: ice harvesting](https://frontenacnews.ca/article.php?id=14630)
- Hand-crank freezer: a metal can inside a wooden tub packed with ice and salt, a crank turning a paddle (dasher) that scrapes the cream off the cold walls; Nancy Johnson's patent, 1843; salt lowers the freezing point so the ice gets colder: [Wikipedia: Ice cream maker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_cream_maker), [Wikipedia: Nancy M. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy_M._Johnson), [Smithsonian: Inventive queens of ices](https://invention.si.edu/inventive-queens-ices)
- Today: mix in tanks, pasteurized (heated to kill germs), homogenized, cooled to 0-4 °C and aged for hours, frozen in a scraped steel barrel while air is whipped in, fruit/chocolate added after, filled into tubs, hardened in a tunnel of blowing cold air, kept frozen: [Food Crumbles: How ice cream is made in a factory](https://foodcrumbles.com/how-ice-cream-is-made-in-a-factory/), [U. Michigan encyclopedia: ice cream](https://encyclopedia.che.engin.umich.edu/ice-cream)
- Taken from (structure only): "How Was Ice Cream Made Before Freezers" (Zack D. Films). Our own angle (lake to bowl, crank freezer, factory), our own wording.
