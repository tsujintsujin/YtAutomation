# Sources


- Tooth key: shaped like a door key, put in sideways, claw tightened over the tooth, turned to loosen it; often broke the tooth, could fracture the jaw; described 1742, in use from about 1730, the most used pulling tool of the late 1700s and early 1800s, replaced by forceps by the late 1800s: [Wikipedia: Dental key](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dental_key), [Royal College of Physicians and Surgeons of Glasgow](https://heritageblog.rcpsg.ac.uk/?p=527), [Science Museum Group](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co98690/dental-key)
- Traveling tooth-pullers who went town to town and worked crowds on market days, with little or no pain relief: [King's College London archives](https://kingscollections.org/exhibitions/archives/dentistry/before-the-lds), [Delaware Academy of Medicine: Dental keys](https://delamed.org/programs/archives-and-history/from-the-archives/dental-keys/)
- Horace Wells, December 1844: breathed nitrous oxide (laughing gas), John Riggs pulled his tooth, "I didn't feel it so much as the prick of a pin": [Wikipedia: Horace Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horace_Wells), [NCBI: Horace Wells](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6908270/)
- Today: X-ray before, local anesthetic shot, gauze behind the tooth as a throat screen, elevator to loosen, forceps rocked side to side then pulled out, bite on gauze for the bleeding: [Merck Manual: tooth extraction](https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/dental-disorders/how-to-do-dental-procedures/how-to-do-an-emergency-tooth-extraction), [Columbia dental syllabus](https://www.columbia.edu/itc/hs/dental/d7710/client_edit/syllabus/toothextraction.html), [Colgate: extraction tools](https://www.colgate.com/en-us/oral-health/procedures/tooth-removal/12-common-tooth-extraction-tools-0217-)
- Taken from (structure only): "Tooth Drilling Before Anesthesia" (Zack D. Films). Different process (pulling, not drilling), our own facts and wording.
