# Sources

- Tallow candles: tallow is rendered animal fat; cotton or flax wicks hung from a candle rod, straightened, dipped in melted tallow, cooled, dipped again until the candle reached the size wanted; earliest were rushlights; snuffers removed the charred wick; candles stored in tin candle boxes, protected from rats and mice; tallow burned unevenly with an offensive smell; a skilled worker could dip about 200 candles a day: [Lakeville Journal: Lighting colonial New England](https://lakevillejournal.com/lighting-colonial-new-england), [Wilton Historical Society: candle dipping](https://wiltonhistorical.org/?p=7368), [Colonial Brewer: basic chandlery](https://colonialbrewer.com/resources/18th-century-skills/basic-chandlery-making-candles/), [Fairfield schools: Colonial candlemaker](https://schcdn.fairfieldschools.org/hh/content/uploads/2017/02/mrnussbaum.com-Colonial-Candlemaker.pdf)
- Today: electricity from power plants over power lines into the house wiring; a switch closes the circuit; an LED bulb's semiconductor chip emits blue light when current flows, a yellow phosphor coating turns it into white light; LEDs run much cooler and last years (DOE: 5 to 10 years depending on use): [Penn State EGEE 102: LED lighting](https://courses.ems.psu.edu/egee102/node/2367), [PacLights: LED bulb longevity](https://www.paclights.com/explore/led-bulb-longevity-energy-efficient-lighting-engineers-guide/)
