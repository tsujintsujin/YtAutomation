# Your light used to be animal fat

In the seventeen hundreds, light at night came from a pot of melted animal fat.

It was called tallow. You melted it in a big pot over the fire. You hung string wicks from a stick, a few in a row. You dipped them into the hot fat. Then you let them cool. Then you dipped them again. And again. Each dip added one thin layer, until the candles were fat enough. A good candle maker could dip about two hundred in a day. But tallow candles smelled bad, and burned unevenly. The wick turned black and curled over, so you snipped it with scissors called snuffers. And you kept your candles in a tin box, safe from rats and mice.

Today, you flick a switch. Electricity comes from a power plant, along wires, into your walls. The switch closes a gap, and the power reaches the bulb. Inside the bulb is a tiny chip. Electricity pushes through it, and it glows blue. A yellow coating turns that blue into white light. It barely gets warm. And one bulb can last for years.

Back then, dip after dip of smelly fat. Now? Flick. Light.

## Why it is built this way

- Template (RULES.md): hook on the surprise (light came from melted animal fat), candle dipping step by step with
  "you", the downsides (smell, snuffers, rats), the now step by step (switch, wires, LED chip, coating), one
  closing line.
- Our own topic (not in the reference Shorts).
- The coloured thing is the light or the fat: the tallow (creamy yellow), the candle flames, then the bulb's glow.
