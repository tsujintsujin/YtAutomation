# Sources

- Sad irons: "sad" is Middle English for "solid"; solid metal irons heated in a fire or on a stove; a set of irons used in turn, a cooled one swapped for a hot one: [Wikipedia: Clothes iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clothes_iron)
- Metal handles got hot, so irons were lifted with a thick cloth and burns were common; Mary Florence Potts of Ottumwa, Iowa, patented a detachable-handle sad iron on April 4, 1871: several iron bodies heated on the stove, one wooden handle clipped onto whichever was hot, a cool handle that saved hands: [Wikipedia: Mary Florence Potts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Florence_Potts), [River Museum: sad iron trivet](https://rivermuseum.org/collection/sad-iron-trivet), [The Henry Ford: Mrs. Potts' Cold Handle Sad Iron trade card](https://www.thehenryford.org/collections-and-research/digital-collections/artifact/108306)
- Electric steam iron: resistive heating element in the soleplate; a thermostat switches the current off and on to hold the set temperature; water from a reservoir fed onto the hot soleplate turns to steam that comes out through holes in the soleplate: [Wikipedia: Clothes iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clothes_iron), [US5446983A: Thermostat control for an electric iron](https://patents.google.com/patent/US5446983), [US3368294A: Steam iron](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3368294A/en)
