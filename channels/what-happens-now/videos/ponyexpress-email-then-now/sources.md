# Sources

- Pony Express: April 3, 1860 to October 1861; about 1,900 miles from St. Joseph, Missouri to Sacramento, California in about 10 days; relay stations about 10 to 15 miles apart where riders changed to a fresh horse; each rider covered about 75 to 100 miles, day and night; the mail rode in a mochila, a leather cover thrown over the saddle with four padlocked pockets; letters first cost $5 per half ounce; it closed when the transcontinental telegraph was finished in October 1861: [Wikipedia: Pony Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pony_Express)
- Email today: the message is turned into digital data and split into packets, each tagged with the destination address and its place in order; packets can travel by different paths and are reassembled in order at the other end; between continents they travel as light through fibre-optic cables on the ocean floor (over 95% of international data): [Illinois MechSE: optical fibers under the sea](https://mechse.illinois.edu/news/blogs/who-lives-optical-fibers-under-sea), [World Economic Forum: undersea cables map](https://www.weforum.org/stories/2016/11/this-map-shows-how-undersea-cables-move-internet-traffic-around-the-world/), [Web Performance Calendar: the shape of the internet](https://calendar.perfplanet.com/2012/the-shape-of-the-internet-is-changing/)
