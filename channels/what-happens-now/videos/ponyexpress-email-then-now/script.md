# A letter across America took ten days on horseback

In eighteen sixty, a letter across America rode on a galloping horse.

You wrote it with a pen you dipped in ink. You folded it into an envelope. Then you paid five dollars to send it, for half an ounce of paper. In Missouri, the Pony Express took it. A rider put it in a leather cover over his saddle, with four locked pockets. He galloped as hard as his horse could go. Every ten to fifteen miles, there was a station. He jumped onto a fresh horse, and kept going. After seventy-five miles or more, a new rider grabbed the leather cover and rode on. Day and night, across deserts and mountains. About ten days later, it reached California. But it only lasted a year and a half. A telegraph wire across the country beat it.

Today, you type an email and press send. Your words turn into ones and zeros. They're split into tiny packets. Each packet gets the address of where it's going. They race through cables as flashes of light. Some cables lie on the bottom of the ocean. Each packet can take a different road. At the other end, they're put back together, in order. And it lands in the inbox in about a second.

Back then, ten days and a lot of tired horses. Now? About one second.

## Why it is built this way

- Template (RULES.md): hook on the picture (a letter riding a galloping horse), the before step by step with "you"
  (write, seal, pay, the relay of horses and riders), how it ended, the now step by step (an email's trip), one
  closing line.
- Our own topic (not in the reference Shorts).
- "Five dollars" is the real starting price per half ounce; the script doesn't guess what that is worth today.

## Lines and pictures

Each line is one picture. The full picture prompts are in `scenes.py` (written into script.json when scenes.py runs). People are plain stick figures, never a likeness of anyone real.

1. **In eighteen sixty,** — The open American West: prairie, a trail, mountains far off.
2. **a letter across America rode on a galloping horse.** — A rider galloping, a letter tucked in the saddle pouch.
3. **You wrote it with a pen you dipped in ink.** — A woman writes a letter with a dip pen and an ink pot.
4. **You folded it into an envelope.** — She folds it into an envelope.
5. **Then you paid five dollars to send it,** — She hands five dollar coins over a post office counter.
6. **for half an ounce of paper.** — The letter on a small scale, tiny next to the coins.
7. **In Missouri, the Pony Express took it.** — A Pony Express station building, a rider waiting with his horse.
8. **A rider put it in a leather cover over his saddle,** — He stuffs the letter into the leather saddle cover.
9. **with four locked pockets.** — Close view: the saddle cover with four pockets and small padlocks.
10. **He galloped as hard as his horse could go.** — The rider galloping flat out, dust flying.
11. **Every ten to fifteen miles, there was a station.** — A small station hut with a corral, a fresh horse waiting.
12. **He jumped onto a fresh horse, and kept going.** — He leaps from the tired horse onto the fresh one.
13. **After seventy-five miles or more,** — The tired rider arrives at a bigger station.
14. **a new rider grabbed the leather cover and rode on.** — A new rider throws the cover onto his saddle and gallops off.
15. **Day and night, across deserts and mountains.** — A rider crossing desert by moonlight, then mountains.
16. **About ten days later, it reached California.** — The rider arrives in Sacramento by the river.
17. **But it only lasted a year and a half.** — The station empty, a "closed" board nailed across the door (no words).
18. **A telegraph wire across the country beat it.** — Telegraph poles with a wire running to the horizon, a rider stopping to stare.
19. **Today, you type an email and press send.** — A young man taps send on his laptop.
20. **Your words turn into ones and zeros.** — The words on screen turning into streams of 1 and 0.
21. **They're split into tiny packets.** — The stream chopped into small boxes.
22. **Each packet gets the address of where it's going.** — Each box gets a small address tag (a house symbol).
23. **They race through cables as flashes of light.** — Light flashes zooming through a see-through cable.
24. **Some cables lie on the bottom of the ocean.** — A cable lying on the sea floor, fish swimming past.
25. **Each packet can take a different road.** — A map of lines with the boxes taking different routes.
26. **At the other end, they're put back together, in order.** — The boxes clicking back into a line, in order.
27. **And it lands in the inbox in about a second.** — A woman's phone lights up with a new message envelope icon.
28. **Back then, ten days and a lot of tired horses.** — A row of tired, panting horses at a station.
29. **Now? About one second.** — The woman reading the message, smiling; a tiny stopwatch.
