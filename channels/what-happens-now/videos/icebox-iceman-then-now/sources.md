# Sources

- Icebox: a block of ice in a compartment near the top, cold air sinks over the food below; hollow walls lined with tin or zinc and packed with cork, sawdust, straw or seaweed; a drip pan underneath that had to be emptied daily; ice bought from an iceman; home refrigerators took over in the 1930s: [Wikipedia: Icebox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icebox)
- Iceman: households put an ice card in the window with the number of pounds wanted (e.g. 25, 50); ice wagons, blocks scored and chipped to size with a pick and weighed, carried with iron tongs on a padded shoulder (sheepskin, leather or rubber pad): [Wikipedia: Iceman (occupation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iceman_(occupation)), [Woods Hole Museum: ice delivery](https://woodsholemuseum.org/icehouse/delivery.html), [History of Black Hawk County: ice delivery](https://historyofblackhawkcounty.uni.edu/econ20thcent/ice_house/delivery.html), [Ephemera Society: The ice man cometh](https://www.ephemerasociety.org/the-ice-man-cometh/)
- Today's fridge: refrigerant in a closed loop; it evaporates in the coils inside and absorbs heat from the food; the compressor squeezes the gas; condenser coils on the back or bottom release the heat into the room; the cycle repeats: [Penn State EGEE 102: Refrigerators](https://courses.ems.psu.edu/egee102/node/2402), [Sears Home Services: How does a refrigerator work](https://www.searshomeservices.com/blog/how-does-a-refrigerator-work-overview)
- Taken from (structure only): "How They Shipped Ice To 1800's India" (Zack D. Films). Different angle (the home icebox and the iceman, then the fridge), our own facts and wording.
