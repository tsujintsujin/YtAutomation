# A man carried ice up your stairs

In nineteen ten, your fridge was a wooden box. And a man carried ice up your stairs to fill it.

It was called an icebox. Its walls were hollow, and stuffed with cork or sawdust. Inside, they were lined with metal. A big block of ice sat in the top. Cold air sank down over the food below.

But the ice melted every day. So you put a card in your window. The number on it told the iceman how many pounds you wanted. His horse and wagon stopped outside. He chipped a block to size with his pick. He grabbed it with big iron tongs and swung it up onto his shoulder. He carried it up to your kitchen and dropped it into the icebox. Under the box sat a pan. It slowly filled with melted ice. And you had to empty it every single day.

Today, your fridge makes its own cold. Hidden pipes run through its walls, filled with a special liquid. Inside, the liquid turns into a gas and soaks up the heat from your food. A pump at the bottom, called a compressor, squeezes that gas. Coils on the back let the heat out into your kitchen. Then it goes round again, all day and all night.

Back then, a block of ice and a man on your stairs. Now? You just plug it in.

## Why it is built this way

- Template (RULES.md): hook on the surprise (the fridge was a wooden box, a man brought the ice), how the icebox
  worked, the iceman step by step, the drip pan, then today's fridge step by step, one closing line.
- Our own angle: the reference Short ships ice to India; ours is the everyday home side, the icebox and the
  iceman at your door, then how a fridge makes cold.
- The window card shows the number "50" in the pictures only (digits are fine in pictures, never in the narration).
