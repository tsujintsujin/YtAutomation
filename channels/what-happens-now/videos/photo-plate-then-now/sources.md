# Sources

- Daguerreotype (introduced 1839, used until about 1856): a silver-plated copper plate polished to a mirror finish; in darkness fumed with iodine (and bromine) to make it light-sensitive; exposure from seconds to minutes (an 1841 guide: 40 to 45 seconds in direct sun); sitters used head rests and posing tables to stay still; developed over heated mercury fumes; fixed with a hot salt solution (Daguerre's first method) or "hypo"; sealed behind cover glass in a case; each plate a single image, laterally reversed (a mirror image): [Wikipedia: Daguerreotype](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daguerreotype)
- Mercury vapour is toxic: [Wikipedia: Mercury poisoning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercury_poisoning)
- Why old portraits rarely smile: long exposures are one of several reasons given, so the script says "probably one reason": [Getty: Why no smiles in old photographs](https://getty.edu/news/why-no-smile-photographs-history-education-question-answer), [Imaging Resource: Why are old portraits so grim](https://www.imaging-resource.com/news/why-are-old-portraits-so-grim)
- Phone camera: the lens focuses light onto an image sensor, a grid of millions of light-sensitive pixels that each measure brightness; a colour filter (red, green or blue) over each pixel; an image signal processor builds, cleans up and saves the photo: [Android Police: How do smartphone cameras work](https://www.androidpolice.com/how-do-smartphone-cameras-work/), [PhoneArena: Bayer filter explained](https://www.phonearena.com/news/Bayer-filter-explained-or-how-your-phones-camera-sees-color_id66281), [TechSpot: smartphone camera hardware](https://www.techspot.com/guides/850-smartphone-camera-hardware/)
