# You used to brush your teeth with soot

In the seventeen hundreds, you cleaned your teeth with a rag, dipped in soot and salt. You rubbed the black, gritty mix over every tooth.

Long before that, people used a chew stick. You chewed the end of a twig until it frayed into a little brush. Then you rubbed it over your teeth. Some scrubbed with powder made from crushed eggshells or burnt shells.

Then, in seventeen seventy, a man named William Addis was thrown in prison. He saved a bone from his dinner. He poked little holes in it, and pushed in tufts of stiff pig hair. After he got out, he started selling brushes just like it. The stiffest hairs came from pigs in cold places, like Siberia.

Today, the bristles are nylon, a kind of plastic. You squeeze on a little blob of toothpaste. It has fluoride, which helps your teeth fight off holes. You brush for two minutes, twice a day. Small circles, on the front, back and top of every tooth. You spit it out, and don't swallow. Rinse the brush, and let it dry. And every three months or so, you get a new one.

Back then, a bone, pig hair, and soot. Now? Plastic bristles and a minty blob.

## Why it is built this way

- Template (RULES.md): hook mid-action (the soot rag), the before going back in time (chew sticks, shell powder),
  then the first brushes made to sell (Addis in prison), the now step by step, one closing line.
- Our own topic (not in the reference Shorts).
- William Addis is named (a real, well-known story) but drawn only as a plain stick figure, never a likeness.
