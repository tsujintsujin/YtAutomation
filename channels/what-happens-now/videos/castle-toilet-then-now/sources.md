# Sources

- Garderobe: castle toilets that projected out of the wall on corbels (stone brackets), a simple hole discharging outside, sometimes down a shaft; the waste fell into the moat, a cesspit or (more rarely) a river: [Wikipedia: Garderobe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garderobe)
- Gong farmers: dug out privies and cesspits, which needed emptying about every two years; worked only at night, nine p.m. to five a.m. (1562 rule); often employed young boys to lift the full buckets; worked "up to his knees, waist, even neck"; the waste had to leave the town and was spread on land or barged out to fertilise fields: [Wikipedia: Gong farmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gong_farmer)
- Today's flush: a burst of water from the tank fills the siphon passage and empties the bowl; the trap's bend always holds water, which blocks sewer gas; the tank refills: [HKU physics: Toilet science](https://www.physics.hku.hk/~phys0607/lectures/chap02.html), [This Old House: how a siphon works](https://www.thisoldhouse.com/plumbing/how-does-a-siphon-work)
- Sewer and treatment: screens catch large debris, grit settles, primary tanks let solids sink as sludge, bacteria (with air pumped in) consume the remaining waste, the water is disinfected and released to the river: [Aspen water recovery: treatment facility](https://www.aspenwaterrecovery.gov/treatment-facility), [Durham Region: wastewater treatment process](https://www.durham.ca/en/living-here/resources/Documents/WaterandSewer/Wastewater-Treatment-Process-Diagram.pdf), [USGS: where does home wastewater go](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/water-qa-where-does-our-home-wastewater-go)
- Taken from (structure only): "How Ancient Toilets Worked" (Zack D. Films). Our own detail on the gong farmer and the whole trip of a flush today, our own wording.
