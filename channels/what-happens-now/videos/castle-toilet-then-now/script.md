# The castle toilet was a hole in the wall

In a medieval castle, the toilet was a little stone room sticking out of the wall. And under the seat? A long drop.

It was called a garderobe. You sat on a stone seat with a hole in it. Everything fell down a shaft in the wall. It landed in the moat, or in a pit at the bottom. The room hung on the outside of the castle, to keep the smell away from everyone.

In towns, people used a privy over a deep pit. Every couple of years, that pit was full. So a gong farmer came to dig it out. He could only work at night, between nine and five. He climbed down into the pit, sometimes up to his waist. Boys hauled up the full buckets. Then he carted it all out of town, to be spread on the fields.

Today, you just push a handle. Water from the tank rushes into the bowl. It pulls everything down a bent pipe. Some water always stays in that bend, to block the smell. The pipe joins a big sewer under the street. It flows to a treatment plant. Screens catch the trash. The heavy stuff sinks to the bottom of big tanks. Tiny bacteria eat up the rest. The clean water goes back into the river.

Back then, a long drop and a man with a bucket. Now? One push, and it's gone.

## Why it is built this way

- Template (RULES.md): hook on the strange picture (a toilet room hanging off a castle wall), the before step by
  step (the garderobe, then the town privy and the gong farmer), the now step by step (flush, trap, sewer,
  treatment plant), one closing line.
- Our own angle: the reference Short covers the castle chute and names the gong farmer; ours adds how the gong
  farmer worked (night hours, the pit, the boys, the fields) and the whole trip of a flush today.
- Nothing crude is shown: the waste is only ever a dark muddy scribble, nobody is shown sitting with bare skin.
